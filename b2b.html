Trade Services · Seattle

Custom fabrication for the trades that keep boats running.

Stuff for Sailors isn't just gear storage for individual boat owners. Behind the scenes, we build precision parts, forms, jigs, and fixtures for the marine trades — fiberglass shops, shipwrights, riggers, canvas and upholstery shops, engine and mechanical shops, and boatyards who need something exact, made fast, without the overhead of setting up their own fabrication shop.

What We Build For Other Trades

Precision parts and fixtures, built from a file.

Fiberglass & Composite Work

Detailed layup forms, mold masters and plugs, vacuum-bagging fixtures, and edge/flange templates — built to your exact geometry from a CAD file, a scan, or a hand-measured sketch.

Dissolvable Forms

For complex geometry — undercuts, internal cavities, anything impossible to pull a form out of once it's cured — we can print the form in a water-soluble material. Lay up and cure as normal, then dissolve it away in a bucket of water instead of fighting it out.

Shipwrights & Boatbuilders

One-off jigs and fixtures for repeatable cuts, drilling patterns, and alignment work — printed once and refined, or made fresh to spec for a job that only happens once.

Riggers

Custom fixtures for terminal fittings, tensioning jigs, and alignment tools for standing and running rigging work. Built to your tolerances, not "close enough."

Marine Electronics & Navigation Installers

Custom mounts, brackets, and enclosures for chartplotters, radar domes, AIS units, autopilot components, antennas, and sensor housings — sized to the exact unit and mounting surface.

Canvas & Upholstery Shops

Custom snap and fastener bases, fitting jigs, pattern templates, and hardware mounts for dodgers, biminis, cushions, and covers — built to the exact curve or mounting point.

Engine & Mechanical Shops

Custom brackets, mounts, jigs, and low-volume replacement parts for engine and mechanical work — including fixtures for repeatable service jobs and one-offs for discontinued parts.

Boatyards

Fixtures and jigs for repair and refit work across trades, mounts and brackets for yard equipment, and one-off replacement parts for the odd jobs that don't fit neatly anywhere else.

Anything Else That Needs a Custom Part

If it doesn't fall neatly into one of the above, ask anyway. Templates, fixtures, low-volume replacement parts, prototypes for a product you're developing — if it can be modeled and printed in a marine-grade material, we can probably build it.

Why Work With Us

Precision, without setting up your own shop.

Precision, From a File, Every Time

We build from CAD, not by eye. Once a part is dialed in, it prints the same way every time — no drift from one copy to the next like you'd get hand-building templates.

Marine-Grade Materials

ASA and carbon-fiber-reinforced filaments for anything exposed to sun and weather, PETG for interior applications. These materials are UV-stable and, unlike metal fittings, inherently corrosion-resistant — no rust, no galvanic corrosion, no worrying about what saltwater does to a fastener or bracket over time.

No Tooling Minimums

One part or fifty, there's no injection-mold setup cost standing between you and a custom piece. That makes this a fit for the one-off jig just as much as a small production run.

No Real Size Limit

A single piece tops out around 256mm (about 10 in) square on our printer, but that's not the ceiling — larger parts get designed as interlocking sections and joined after printing, so a full-size layup form or a large jig is still very much on the table.

Fast Iteration

Print it, test-fit it, adjust the file, print again. If the first version isn't quite right, the fix is a file edit and a reprint — not starting over from raw material.

Your Design Stays Yours

Bringing us a proprietary jig, fixture, or part design? We can sign an NDA before you share any files or specs.

We Speak Your Language

My natural tendency to spot the ergonomic problem, the wasted inch of space, the fitting that almost works — and then build the thing that actually solves it — is what led me to start Stuff for Sailors: a home- (boat-) based shop built around boaters' ongoing storage challenges. I now offer that same problem-solving to the marine trades.

Whether it was developing procedures as a classically trained chef, running the stern of a lobster boat off the coast of Maine, working Washington farmland, building custom timber-frame homes, or running and maintaining a small marina, I've always seemed to have a tool of some sort in my hands. That range means I generally understand the job you're describing — and the constraints you're working under — before you finish explaining it.

How It Works

From job to installed part, in four steps.

1

Tell Us What You Need

2

We Model It

3

Prototype & Confirm Fit

4

Production

One part or a batch — printed in the material suited to how and where it'll be used.
